3b.2  SELECTION vs EXPOSURE -- unbiased decomposition
================================================================================================
Method: regress the strategy's GROSS daily return on the equal-weight market
daily return over all 685 TEST bars. No conditioning, so no exposure bias.

  market (equal-weight universe) daily mean   3.617 bp
  strategy gross daily mean               0.944 bp
  average exposure                     0.3004

  regression:  strategy_gross = alpha + beta * market
    beta   0.338   (market exposure actually taken)
    alpha  -0.277 bp/day   t = -0.00
    annualised alpha  -0.68 pp/yr  (se 405.05)
    -> alpha indistinguishable from zero

Reading:
  beta 0.34 confirms the book is structurally under-exposed: it participates
  in roughly 34% of the market's daily move, because the sparse gate plus the
  12% single-name cap leave ~70% of the book in cash.
  alpha -0.68 pp/yr (t=-0.00) is the separate question of
  whether the names the gate DOES pick beat the universe.

Compare the strategy's gross return against beta*market at the SAME time,

  mean(strategy - 0.34*market) =  -0.277 bp/day
  t-statistic                        =  -0.19
  annualised                         =  -0.68 pp/yr (se 3.48)
  -> selection edge is statistically indistinguishable from zero.
     The Donchian gate carries NO positive stock-selection information in TEST.

================================================================================================
3b.4  WHY IS THE BOOK 70% CASH? -- the sparsity arithmetic
================================================================================================
  rebalance dates in TEST        : 34
  names flagged, mean            : 3.4
  names flagged, median          : 2
  names in the tradable universe : ~47
  rebalances with 0 flags        : 7  -> 100% cash that month
  rebalances with <=3 flags      : 23  -> 3*12% = 36% max investable

  The catalog's breakouts are RARE events. A strategy that only holds names on
  the rare days it is confident is, for a long-only delivery account, a strategy
  that is in cash 70% of the time. The 12% single-name cap then makes it worse:
  you cannot concentrate into the 2-3 names you like to make up for the cash.

  This is a mismatch of signal FREQUENCY with PORTFOLIO STRUCTURE, not evidence
  that breakouts never work. It is a design flaw in adapting this per-symbol
  +1/0/-1 strategy to a 22-name cross-sectional book.
